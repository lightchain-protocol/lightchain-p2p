        Availability

        Blind peers are always-on machines that hold your rooms without being able to read them. Without one, a room only replicates while someone who has it is online — so two people who are never online together never see each other's messages. There is no public fleet; these are machines you or someone you trust runs.

        Give this key to whoever runs the blind peer. It must be on their trusted list, or they will hold your rooms and never advertise them — which looks like it is working until the last person closes the app.

        Where rooms, the wallet vault and these settings live on this machine. Anyone who can read this directory can read your rooms.

                            How this room is protected
                            
            
                            
              What each layer protects, what performs it, and where its specification is.
            
                            
          
                            
          
                            
            
          
                            
        
                            

        
                            
          
          
        
                            

        
                            
          A room has two keys. The room key says which room this is; the encryption key is what
          reads it. They are handed over together in a pairing confirmation and never apart, which
          is why a blind peer can hold this room and keep it available without being able to read a
          word of it.
        
                            

        
                            
          
                            
            
            
                            Where it sits on this disk
                            
            XSalsa20
          
                            
          
                            
            
                            Protects
                            
            
                            
              Everything ever said in this room, in the folder on this machine. Copying that folder
              — or being the blind peer that holds it — gets you ciphertext.
            
                            
            
                            Mechanism
                            
            
                            
              Hypercore's default block encryption. Every block goes through libsodium's
              crypto_stream_xor, which is XSalsa20, under a block key that BLAKE2b
              derives from this room's encryption key and the core's public key. The eight bytes of
              padding in front of each block are blinded under a second key derived from the first.
            
                            
          
                            
          
                            
            No authentication tag. A stream cipher conceals a block; it cannot tell you a byte
            changed. Detection belongs to the layer above it — a BLAKE2b Merkle tree over the log,
            with an Ed25519 signature over the root.
          
                            
          
                            
            Specifications
            
            
            
          
                            
        
                            

        
                            
          
                            
            
            
                            On the way to another peer
                            
            Noise XX
          
                            
          
                            
            
                            Protects
                            
            
                            
              Every byte that leaves this machine, including the replication traffic carrying the
              room. A relay, a node on the DHT, or anything sitting between two peers, sees
              ciphertext.
            
                            
            
                            Mechanism
                            
            
                            
              @hyperswarm/secret-stream. A connection opens with a Noise XX handshake
              — ChaCha20-Poly1305 for the handshake messages, BLAKE2b for the hashing, Ed25519
              static keys through noise-curve-ed. The session that follows runs on
              XChaCha20-Poly1305 through libsodium's secretstream. It is a separate layer from the
              one above: the room's own encryption is still underneath it.
            
                            
          
                            
          
                            
            A handshake proves the far end holds a particular key. It does not say who is holding
            it. Which people belong in a room is settled by the invite that let them in, not by the
            transport.
          
                            
          
                            
            Specifications
            
            
            
          
                            
        
                            

        
                            
          
                            
            
            
                            The wallet on this machine
                            
            scrypt + AES-256-GCM
          
                            
          
                            
            
                            Protects
                            
            
                            
              The recovery phrase this machine signs with — not the room. A stolen vault file is an
              offline guessing problem, not a readable conversation.
            
                            
            
                            Mechanism
                            
            
                            
              scrypt stretches the password into a 32-byte key at N = 262,144, r = 8, p = 1, and
              AES-256-GCM encrypts the phrase under it. Those parameters are written into the vault
              rather than assumed, so they can be raised later without orphaning wallets that
              already exist.
            
                            
          
                            
          
                            
            The password is the weak part, not the cipher. Nobody is going to break AES-256-GCM;
            somebody holding the file will guess passwords offline, and all scrypt does is make
            each guess cost memory.
          
                            
          
                            
            Specifications
            
            
          
                            
        
                            

        
                            
          
                            
            
            
                            Who wrote what
                            
            EIP-191
          
                            
          
                            
            
                            Protects
                            
            
                            
              The name on a message. Encryption keeps strangers out of the room; it says nothing
              about which member wrote a given line inside it.
            
                            
            
                            Mechanism
                            
            
                            
              An EIP-191 signature from the author's wallet, over a preimage naming this room's
              key, the message id, the writer, the clock and a hash of the whole entry. This
              machine recovers an address from that signature and compares it with the one the
              message claims. Naming the room key is what stops a signature being lifted out of
              this conversation and replayed into another.
            
                            
          
                            
          
                            
          
                            
            A message with no signature is not a forgery. It predates signing, or came from a peer
            with no wallet, so it is shown unattributed rather than thrown away.
          
                            
          
                            
            Specifications
            
          
                            
        
                            

        
        
                            
          
                            
            
            
                            What none of this hides
                            
          
                            
          
                            
            Encryption protects what was said. It does not protect the shape of the conversation
            around it, and the four things below are visible without decrypting anything.
          
                            
          
                              
            
                            • 
              That this room exists. Peers find each other by announcing the
              room's discovery key — a BLAKE2b hash of the room key — on a public DHT. The hash
              does not give up the key, but anyone watching that topic learns a room is there and
              that somebody is looking for it.
            
                              • 
            
                            • 
              Who is connected to it. Connections are direct wherever they can be,
              so a peer learns your IP address, and the DHT sees which addresses announce this
              topic. None of that is anonymised. This is an encrypted tool, not an anonymity tool.
            
                              • 
            
                            • 
              When each message was written. Entries land in an append-only log in
              order. A blind peer that cannot read a single block still watches new blocks arrive,
              and when.
            
                              • 
            
                            • 
              Roughly how long each message was. The block cipher preserves length
              and pads by a fixed eight bytes, so ciphertext size tracks plaintext size. A one-word
              reply and a pasted document are distinguishable without reading either.
