Settings

Network

Which chain balances are read from and transactions are signed for. Changing it does not move anything - a wallet has the same address on every network. Devnet is for trying things out: asking models works, but it has no explorer and no worker hosting yet.

Locking

How long the wallet stays open with nothing happening. It locks itself after that, and opening it again costs your password. Rooms close with it.

Privacy

A read receipt tells the other people in a room when you have seen what they wrote - it is what turns the tick on their messages into two. That is a fact about you, not about the conversation, so it is off unless you switch it on. Receipts travel over the presence channel and are written nowhere: nothing about what you have read ever enters a room's history. Off, the rooms you are in are told nothing, and other people's switches decide what their ticks tell you - never the other way round.

Notifications

When one of the balances this wallet watches grows - a deposit landing, on any of the six networks - a message says what arrived and on which chain. The message shows either way; this switch is only about the sound that comes with it. On by default, because money arriving is news worth noticing.

Recovery phrase

Twelve words that restore this wallet anywhere. Seeing them costs your password, and anyone who has them has the wallet.

    Change your password

    Reseals the wallet on this machine. Your recovery phrase, your address and your saved conversations are unaffected - the phrase is what your wallet is, and the password only guards the copy kept here.

    Remove this wallet

    Deletes the encrypted vault from this machine. Without your recovery phrase written down, this cannot be undone by anyone.

    Spending limits

    Both caps are optional and both are in LCAI. A job costs about 0.02 LCAI on mainnet, which is the figure worth holding in mind when choosing one. Leave a field empty for no limit.

    These are checked by this application, on this machine, before it submits a job. They will catch a typo, a loop that keeps asking and a model that costs more than you expected. They will not stop anybody who controls this machine, because anything that can bypass this window can bypass the check with it. The delegate allowance you authorised on chain is the only limit that is enforced against your money.

    Worker

    Used by the Worker section to talk to a running worker. Anything left blank falls back to the environment variables the toolkit already uses, so an existing shell setup keeps working.

    Prompt templates

    A template is a block of text put in front of a prompt - the standing instructions you would otherwise retype every time. Templates are sealed under your wallet and kept on this machine only: no peer, no worker and no contract is ever told that this list exists.

      Network trust

      Who runs what. The workers that answer questions are run by anyone, and this app does not choose them. The parts in between - the dispatcher that routes a question to a worker, the relay that carries the answer back, the disputer that settles challenges, and the service that puts the question's data on chain - are all run by the Lightchain foundation, one operator.

      What is checked. Every answer carries the worker's signature, and this app verifies it before showing anything, so an answer cannot be swapped for another on the way. A worker that tells you one thing while recording something else on chain has equivocated, and that can be disputed - it costs the worker its stake. What no check can catch is an answer that is wrong but reads as right: a model that is simply mistaken looks exactly like one that is not, so judge answers accordingly.

      What the prepaid balance means. Funding it also authorises the delegate - the service that submits jobs for you - to spend it, and that authorisation stands until you revoke it on chain. Withdrawing what is left does not revoke it, so a later deposit is spendable again without asking. The delegate can reach the prepaid balance and nothing else; the rest of the wallet is never part of the arrangement.

      And one thing about this window. The dialog that asks you to confirm a transfer is drawn by this application, not by the operating system. It vouches for what this app is about to sign and nothing wider - it cannot see, and says nothing about, anything else running on this machine.

      Availability

      Blind peers are always-on machines that hold your rooms without being able to read them. Without one, a room only replicates while someone who has it is online - so two people who are never online together never see each other's messages. There is no public fleet; these are machines you or someone you trust runs.

      Give this key to whoever runs the blind peer. It must be on their trusted list, or they will hold your rooms and never advertise them - which looks like it is working until the last person closes the app.

      Storage

      Where rooms, the wallet vault and these settings live on this machine. Anyone who can read this directory can read your rooms.

      Diagnostics

      One ZIP to hand to whoever is helping with a broken machine: the recent logs, the version and the host-readiness probe summary - never keys, seed words or messages, which are left out by never being read at all.
